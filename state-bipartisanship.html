State Legislative Bipartisanship Scores

Mackenzie R. Dobson · University of Tennessee, Knoxville · State Politics & Policy Quarterly, 2026

Original measures of bipartisan cosponsorship behavior for 10,817 legislators serving in 43 U.S. state legislatures between 2009 and 2018. Two scores are computed at the legislator-term level: offered (the proportion of a legislator's cosponsorships directed to out-party bills) and attracted (the average proportion of out-party cosponsors on a legislator's own bills). Developed to test the theory of selective reciprocity in Dobson (2026), State Politics & Policy Quarterly 26(2): 139–164.

Measures

Both scores are computed at the legislator-term level from bill-level cosponsorship records and scaled 0–1. They capture distinct dimensions of bipartisan engagement — extending support to the other side versus attracting it.

Bipartisan cosponsorships offered

Bipartisan cosponsors attracted

Distribution of bipartisan cosponsorships offered

Each curve shows how the proportion of cosponsorships given to out-party bills is distributed across individual legislator-term observations within each group, pooled across the full 2009–2018 study period. The rightward shift of minority party curves — and the growing divergence between minority and majority in secure chambers — illustrates the asymmetry central to the theory of selective reciprocity.

Each curve pools all legislator-term observations in the respective group across 2009–2018. Distributions approximated across 20 equal-width bins spanning 0–1. N = 27,129 legislator-term observations.
